ECO Receipt

A lightweight AI web app that turns a photo of a grocery receipt — in almost any language — into a carbon footprint, a Green Score and a couple of simple swaps for the next shop.

(01)

The problem

Most people never connect a weekly grocery run with carbon emissions. Climate talk centres on factories and fuel, while everyday consumption quietly adds up. Existing GreenTech tools lean B2B — checkout integrations, donation round-ups, manual searches — so the everyday shopper is left without a simple way to see what a basket really costs the planet.

01 · Problem

Shoppers can’t see it

  • A receipt lists every price, never the carbon
  • Climate talk centres on factories and fuel, not baskets
  • Existing tools need manual entry or a retailer partnership
02 · Why

Why it matters

  • Food systems drive about a third of global greenhouse gas emissions
  • Item choice matters: a kilo of beef emits about 31× more than a kilo of tofu
  • Thermal receipts are thrown away within minutes
03 · Goal

Objectives

  • One photo in, a footprint out — no install, no account
  • Numbers people can feel and swaps they can actually make
  • Any receipt, from any store, in almost any language

Sources: Crippa et al., Nature Food (2021); Poore & Nemecek, Science (2018), via Our World in Data.

(02)

Who it’s for

Everyday shoppers

People who buy groceries every week and have never seen their basket expressed in CO₂e.

Curious beginners

Students and first-timers who want to learn without installing anything or reading a report.

Households & families

Changing habits one shop at a time, with swaps that fit local food — like tempeh in place of beef.

Travellers & expats

Shopping abroad? The receipt can be in Japanese, Thai or German; the result comes back in English or Bahasa Indonesia.

(04)

What I designed

Receipt-to-result flow

Camera or gallery upload, local image compression to save data, then a result screen with total CO₂e, a Green Score, the highest-emission items and clear advice.

Atmosphere system

The score drives the whole UI: glassmorphism over a living forest, weather and smoke particles on canvas, and generative soundscapes from the Web Audio API.

Safe, forgiving AI

The model is prompted to ignore non-product lines and to reject non-receipt images such as faces or landscapes, instead of inventing results.

Two modes & two languages

Live AI for the public, plus a Dev / Demo mode with low, mid and high samples, so anyone can try the full flow — in English or Bahasa Indonesia.

(05)

How I approached it

01 — Frame the audience

Chose B2C education over enterprise dashboards: change behaviour in the pocket, at the moment of purchase.

02 — Scan the field

Benchmarked checkout tools and footprint calculators — mostly B2B, manual entry or API-reliant — to find the gap.

03 — Design the feedback loop

Made the result felt, not just read: colour, motion and sound that match how the basket scored.

04 — Prototype with AI

Took the concept straight to a working, coded MVP with Gemini as a coding partner, then tuned the prompts and the interface until the flow felt effortless.

(10)

Reading the result

A number you can feel

Total CO₂e in large serif type, translated into something familiar: 8.26 kg CO₂e is “about the same as driving 49 km in a petrol car.”

A score with a mood

A Green Score out of 100 with a plain label — 84 “Very eco-friendly”, 58 “Fairly good”, 14 “High emissions” — while the whole card shifts from forest green to amber to ember red.

Highest emissions first

The item breakdown is sorted by impact, each row with weight, category and a bar. The long tail folds away behind “Show more items”.

Advice that fits the basket

Three AI suggestions grounded in what was bought — when beef dominates: swap one serving a week for tempeh or tofu and save about 50 kg CO₂e a month.

(11)

Honest by design

Still a prototype

A first-run notice says it plainly: emission figures are AI estimates, not verified measurements.

Privacy up front

Receipt photos go to Gemini for analysis only. No accounts, no stored copy — and a tip to avoid receipts showing names, phone or card numbers.

Sample data is labelled

A “Demo mode: sample data” badge keeps anyone from mistaking a demo result for a real one.

(12)

Built with

Front end

Pure HTML, CSS and vanilla JavaScript with Tailwind — light enough to load fast on any phone.

Motion & sound

GSAP for transitions, HTML5 canvas for smoke and weather, Web Audio API for real-time soundscapes.

AI & hosting

Google Gemini’s multimodal model with an automatic model fallback, behind a small Cloud Run proxy; the app itself runs on Firebase Hosting.

(13)

Where it could go next

Memory over time

Accounts and history, so shoppers can watch their footprint fall week by week.

Connected receipts

Supermarket loyalty and e-receipt integrations, so no photo is needed at all.

Habits & community

Weekly goals, streaks and shared challenges to keep the nudge going.

Still an MVP — a working proof that a receipt photo can become a small, useful lesson.
